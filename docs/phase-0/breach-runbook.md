# Security compromise response — draft

Status: prepare before production; information officer and on-call contacts unassigned.

1. Record detection time, incident identifier, affected systems and evidence. Restrict the incident channel and do not paste personal records into tickets.
2. Contain exposed credentials/links and affected access paths. Preserve immutable evidence; do not delete evidence in an attempt to hide the incident.
3. Determine affected hubs, employers, people, data and processing locations. Investigate availability/integrity impact as well as disclosure. Inform the affected responsible party immediately through the agreed incident contact.
4. The information officer/legal lead assesses notification duties and content. Record decisions and reasons; do not invent a fixed statutory deadline from another jurisdiction.
5. Coordinate notification to the Information Regulator and affected people as required. Confirm the current submission process before filing. The Regulator's published guidance directs security-compromise reporting through its eServices portal.
6. Restore safely, rotate credentials, revalidate tenant boundaries, and reconcile queued commerce/access events. Apply deletion tombstones after restoring backups.
7. Track remediation owner, due date and verification. Review the runbook and contractual processor obligations after the incident.

Required before go-live: named information officer, provider/employer escalation contacts, on-call rota, regulator access/process, evidence storage, notification templates and a rehearsal.

Source: [Information Regulator POPIA guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/).
